# Act 1, Scene 1: The Leaking Skylight

*Setting: The central tiled courtyard (muttram) of an old heritage home on Monsoon Street. Rain drums steadily on the clay roof tiles. Four brass vessels and one brightly mismatched blue plastic bucket sit strategically beneath drips falling from the timber rafters. Along the veranda, glass-fronted bookshelves groan under bound volumes.*

[ARJUN kneels beside the blue bucket, holding a vinyl sleeve over his head like a miniature roof while trying to rescue a stack of Tamil literary magazines. KAVYA enters from the portico, shaking out a measuring tape and a soaked blueprint tube.]

KAVYA
If you keep moving that blue bucket two inches to the left every time it splashes, the teak beam is going to rot right above grandfather's dictionaries.

ARJUN
[Without looking up, sliding a brass vessel under a fresh drip]
It isn't rotting, Kavya, it's singing in C-sharp minor. Listen to the brass pot versus the plastic bucket. You want to demolish an acoustic masterpiece for a parking lot.

KAVYA
[Setting her soggy bag on the carved swing]
I didn't come from Bengaluru to build a parking lot. I came because Raghav says the municipal tax notice is thicker than *Ponniyin Selvan*, and nobody has fixed the skylight flashing since 2004.

[MEENAKSHI emerges from the kitchen corridor carrying two stainless-steel tumblers of steaming filter coffee, stepping neatly around the puddles as though she had choreographed them.]

MEENAKSHI
First drink the coffee before you start dividing the roof tiles. Your grandfather built this courtyard so rain would fall inside the house—just not onto the sofa.

ARJUN
[Accepting a tumbler gratefully]
Tell her, Athai. A house should be allowed to leak a little in November. It keeps people honest.

KAVYA
[Smiling slightly despite herself, taking the other tumbler]
Athai, where is Raghav? His train reached Egmore an hour ago.

MEENAKSHI
[Glancing toward the wooden front door as thunder rolls softly overhead]
He stopped at the notary's office on Luz Church Road. Apparently your father left one more envelope inside the safe—marked *To be opened only when all three of you are standing under the same roof.*

[A sudden gust blows rain sideways across the veranda, rattling the glass bookcases. ARJUN and KAVYA exchange an uneasy look.]
