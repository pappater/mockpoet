# Before the Lamps Are Lit

There is an hour between the last crow's call
and the first yellow window on the hill
when the grass asks nothing of your name.

You may stand in your ordinary coat,
hands deep in the pockets where yesterday's receipts
have softened into lint,
and listen to the creek practice its single syllable
over wet slate.

Look how effortlessly the heron folds
its gray architecture into the reeds.
Not everything that endures must announce itself;
some mercies arrive simply by staying still
until the dark feels familiar.
