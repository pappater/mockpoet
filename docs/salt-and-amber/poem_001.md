# Inventory of a Coastal Kitchen

Three chipped bowls stacked by size,
a tin of smoked paprika dented at the seam,
and the kettle that whistles half a note flat
whenever the tide turns inward under the pilings.

On the windowsill, a jar of sea glass—
milk-white, bottle-green, one stubborn triangle
of cobalt blue the surf spent twenty winters
learning how to forgive.

Nothing here belongs to haste.
Even the salt in the wooden cellar
remembers the patience of evaporation,
crystal by quiet crystal,
waiting for the hand that knows
how little of anything is needed to taste the sea.
