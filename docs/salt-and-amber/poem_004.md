# Salt & Amber

At dusk my mother rinses rice in an aluminum pot,
the cloudy water swirling like a small galaxy
before she tips it toward the garden mint.

Between one breath and the next,
she hums a tune older than the highway outside our fence—
a melody bruised with ginger, rain, and salt,
preserved like pine resin around a summer moth.

I learn to listen with my shoulders lowered.
What we carry across oceans is rarely luggage;
it is the weight of a spoon resting against ceramic,
the amber warmth of a doorway left unlocked.
