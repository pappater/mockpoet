# Act 1, Scene 1: The Stopped Pendulum

*Setting: ARTHUR VANCE's watchmaking workshop above Ravenscar Junction. Rain streaks the arched window overlooking the wet slate roof of Platform 1. On the workbench, a dozen brass movements tick out of phase—except for one tall walnut regulator clock against the far wall, whose pendulum hangs motionless. A brass desk lamp casts an amber pool over scattered loupes and tiny screwdrivers.*

[ARTHUR stands motionless before the walnut regulator clock, holding a silver pocket watch in his palm. Outside, a distant steam whistle fades into the fog. Footsteps climb the wooden stairs.]

CLARA
[Entering with a damp wool coat over her shoulders and a folded telegraph slip in hand]
You left the street latch unbolted again, Arthur. Anyone walking off the 8:40 could have wandered straight up into your escapements.

ARTHUR
[Not turning immediately, closing his fingers over the pocket watch]
No one walks off the 8:40 in this weather unless they have business with the timetable, Clara. Or with the police.

CLARA
[Pausing near the workbench, shaking rain from her umbrella into the brass stand]
Then you already heard about Julian Mercer.

ARTHUR
[Turning slowly, placing the silver watch face-down beside a tray of balance wheels]
The stationmaster came up at seven to ask if my window commands a view of the coal siding. A clockmaker is apparently useful only when someone wishes to prove where they were not standing.

CLARA
Inspector Hargreaves arrived from York twenty minutes ago. He asked me for the telegraph log from Tuesday night—every dispatch between nine and ten o'clock.

ARTHUR
And did you give it to him?

CLARA
I gave him the official ledger. [She steps closer to the workbench, lowering her voice.] What I did not hand over yet is the scrap ribbon that jammed under my key at 9:11. Someone sent an uncoded message from the platform booth three minutes before the express cleared the bridge.

ARTHUR
[Looking up sharply]
At 9:11?

CLARA
[Pointing toward the silent walnut clock behind him]
Look at your regulator, Arthur. You haven't let that pendulum stop since your wife died. Why is it resting at eleven minutes past nine?

[A heavy knock sounds on the street door downstairs. Both freeze as a faint vibration from a passing freight train makes the glass bell jars on the shelf hum softly.]

ARTHUR
[Quietly, slipping the silver watch into his waistcoat pocket]
Go to the tea shelf, Clara. Put the kettle on, and whatever the Inspector asks about Tuesday night—let him look at the clocks before we tell him which one is lying.
