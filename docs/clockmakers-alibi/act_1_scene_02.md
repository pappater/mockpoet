# Act 1, Scene 2: The Platform Ticket

*Setting: The same workshop, five minutes later. A kettle whistles softly on a small spirit stove. INSPECTOR HARGREAVES stands near the foggy window, his leather notebook open, eyeing the motionless pendulum of the walnut regulator while CLARA pours tea into mismatched cups.*

HARGREAVES
A curious profession, Mr. Vance. You spend forty years teaching brass wheels to agree on the minute, yet the largest clock in your room refuses to move.

ARTHUR
[Polishing a magnifying loupe with a chamois cloth]
A clock only stops for two reasons, Inspector: either someone forgot to wind it, or someone wished to preserve a moment without arguing with it.

HARGREAVES
[Turning from the window, pulling a damp cardboard square from his coat pocket]
And which of those explains this? Found wedged beneath the floorboards of the first-class carriage on Tuesday's 9:14 express—after Julian Mercer vanished between here and Whitby.

[He lays a soot-smudged platform ticket onto the green baize mat of the workbench.]

CLARA
[Setting down the teapot, leaning slightly to read the stamp]
A platform ticket? Not a travel fare?

HARGREAVES
Issued at 9:06 PM at Ravenscar Junction. Someone paid twopence merely to walk onto Platform 1 six minutes before the express arrived—never intending to board. Or intending to meet someone who never meant to leave.

ARTHUR
[Examining the corner of the ticket through his loupe without touching it]
There is oil on the left margin. Not locomotive grease—watchmaker's bone oil.

HARGREAVES
[Raising an eyebrow]
Sharp eyes, Mr. Vance. Do you happen to sell twopenny tickets between mainspring repairs?

ARTHUR
No, Inspector. [He looks steadily toward the shadowed doorway leading to the back storeroom.] But my apprentice Tomas keeps his coat hung beside the oil shelf—and he has not come up the stairs since Tuesday night.

[From the back storeroom, a floorboards creaks faintly. CLARA glances toward the doorway, concealing the folded telegraph slip inside her sleeve.]

HARGREAVES
[Closing his notebook with a crisp snap]
Then I suggest we invite young Tomas to explain why a watchmaker's apprentice needed an alibi on Platform 1 at nine minutes past nine.
